## What changed

- All matter records have exact-topic guidance or an existing deeper editorial profile. Document sections match the issue being assessed. There are no identical full FAQ-answer sets across different matters within the same country, service and language. Existing canonical paths are preserved; 264 redundant UAE procedural URLs remain permanently consolidated into 44 language-matched service pages.
- Every service has a subject-specific introduction and evidence checklist. Saudi/Syrian coverage is aligned between languages, UAE related-service links follow the subject, and the authority question distinguishes statutory competence from contractual dispute clauses.
- All 45 article pairs have complete summaries; 41 article records have body changes, including the distinct commercial-evidence guide and correction of the Syrian interpretation placeholder. Identified legal propositions were checked against the cited primary sources. Article relationships follow their actual subject and jurisdiction. General commentary is not forced into Saudi jurisdiction merely to create a link.
- All 45 work records have reviewed relationships. Missing English fields and two English routes are restored from the existing source narratives. Corrections preserve figures, liability distinctions and procedural stages; later authored CMS fields are protected by legacy-value or source-fingerprint guards.
- Current source and terminology corrections include Saudi investor registration and Insurance Authority complaints, Syrian copyright/investment/cybercrime references, Dubai Rental Disputes Center naming and UAE Capital Market Authority wording. Syrian tax labels distinguish local tax review from an explicitly foreign VAT issue.
- The actual API renderer serves complete article and work bodies in initial HTML. The standalone React renderer also bootstraps the full matching detail record so hydration preserves the article/work content. Existing prerender files cannot keep a removed article or work record alive. Temporary content failures return 503 with retry guidance; confirmed removals return 404.
- The build and both servers now share all 43 legacy blog redirect contracts, fixing the 36 destination mismatches exposed by the HTTP sweep. Replit's build generates shared TypeScript definitions before checking the applications.

Google prioritizes useful, reliable, people-first content; Bing also emphasizes clarity and avoiding low-value duplication.
