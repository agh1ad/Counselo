# Legal Problem Page Accuracy and Conversion Audit

**Audit date:** 2026-08-18
**Scope:** 692 legal-problem records and their 1,384 English/Arabic routes across Saudi Arabia, Syria and the UAE.
**Release classification:** framework-verified, matter-specific review required.

## Executive result

The legal-problem content now meets the repository's 10/10 publication-readiness rubric for legal-safety boundaries and conversion architecture. This is not a claim that every visitor has a particular entitlement, that a legal outcome is guaranteed, or that the pages have achieved a measured 10/10 conversion rate. Substantive advice still requires the operative text, relevant date, facts, documents and competent forum to be checked for the individual matter.

Every page now:

1. identifies the facts, evidence and objective relevant to the searched problem;
2. describes the legal framework as potentially applicable until verified;
3. separates intake from an accepted professional engagement;
4. warns that contact does not suspend or extend a deadline;
5. states the jurisdiction-specific checks that can change the answer;
6. promises only a deliverable within the agreed scope, not a result;
7. gives the visitor a four-item intake checklist;
8. exposes contact and WhatsApp actions in the hero, sidebar and closing section;
9. links the parent service, related problems and official authority starting points; and
10. publishes a review date and machine-tested content status.

## Readability and hierarchy revision

The shared template now prioritizes the visitor's decision path rather than the full content inventory:

1. a concise problem summary and two primary actions appear in the hero;
2. a three-card “at a glance” block identifies the issue, useful evidence and expected output;
3. sticky navigation is limited to five choices: overview, legal checks, deliverable, documents and contact;
4. experience is consolidated into the trust strip instead of repeated in a standalone section;
5. detailed questions, process, paid-package contents and sources remain available in disclosure panels without dominating the initial scan; and
6. lower-page sections use deferred rendering hints to reduce rendering work on the long template.

English desktop and Arabic RTL mobile layouts must retain this sequence in rendered QA. Regression tests fail if the hero summary, at-a-glance block, five-link navigation or disclosure structure is removed.

## Main service hub revision

The same publication standard now applies to the 118 bilingual main-service routes. Their role is to help a visitor choose the right legal problem and start a properly scoped review, not to provide a predetermined legal answer.

The shared service template now:

1. places contact-form and WhatsApp actions in the hero;
2. replaces promotional hero claims with a jurisdiction-qualified assessment summary;
3. opens with a three-card issue, evidence and output summary;
4. limits sticky navigation to overview, legal checks, common problems, documents and contact;
5. places legal-verification and deadline boundaries before the detailed service inventory;
6. links every registered problem page instead of showing unlinked issue labels;
7. uses a neutral four-stage process that confirms scope and avoids promised outcomes;
8. keeps the package and broader service explanation in optional disclosure panels;
9. uses the safe universal FAQ set on service hubs, leaving proposition-specific answers to the audited problem pages; and
10. records the 18 August 2026 source-routing verification date in page metadata and visible content.

Legacy promotional narratives remain in translation data for other surfaces, but the main-service template no longer renders their outcome-oriented overview, experience, process or proposition-specific FAQ fields. Regression tests enforce that boundary.

## Remaining public page families

The same trust standard is applied according to page purpose rather than by duplicating the legal-problem layout everywhere:

- regional home pages explain jurisdiction, service scope and conversion paths;
- service directories help visitors select a practice area and now use neutral, scoped descriptions instead of legacy promotional narratives;
- About and Vision pages explain identity, governance and operating values without unsupported ranking or trust superlatives;
- Contact pages explain intake, timing, scope and confidentiality before submission;
- blog and Legal Library pages expose authorship, review information, content basis, correction routes and related discovery;
- Our Work pages identify redaction and state that past work or outcomes do not guarantee another result; and
- Terms and Privacy pages prioritize complete policy text and direct navigation rather than adopting a sales-page hierarchy.

The English and Arabic region selectors and regional home pages now use qualified, jurisdiction-scoped positioning. Accessible H1 text retains spaces across visual line breaks. The Vision page no longer claims to be or become the “most trusted” legal reference, and its confidentiality wording is qualified by applicable professional, privacy and data-protection obligations.

## Primary-authority verification

The current source review used official government sources:

- Saudi Arabia: the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development currently publishes the Labour Law and showed a 17 June 2026 update during this audit. The Saudi Bureau of Experts remains the official laws-portal starting point used in the source matrix.
- UAE: the UAE Legislation platform identifies itself as the official unified and updated government legislation platform. Its official record includes Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2024 issuing the Personal Status Law. Known UAE services retain their topic-specific legislation or regulator link.
- Syria: the People's Assembly remains the legislative authority starting point. The Ministry of Justice website returned a maintenance page during this audit, so Syrian current-text verification cannot be represented as complete from that site alone.

Official-source routing is evidence of provenance, not proof that a provision applies to a visitor's facts. The new on-page boundary says this directly.

## Automated controls

The legal-problem registry tests now fail if a page:

- lacks the 2026-08-18 review date or framework-verified status;
- lacks three jurisdiction-specific legal checks in either language;
- lacks the deadline and no-engagement warnings;
- lacks the four-item intake checklist;
- omits potentially-applicable or agreed-scope language from the deliverable; or
- introduces outcome-guarantee wording.

The broader claims tests continue to reject known Saudi legal inaccuracies, unsupported endorsements, unverified testimonial outcomes, unsupported licence numbers and unqualified professional-role claims.

## Human legal sign-off boundary

Automated review can verify route coverage, consistency, provenance, risky wording and whether the content avoids unsupported legal propositions. It cannot certify the legal answer to 692 categories without a qualified lawyer checking the operative legislation and facts for each individual matter. CounselO should keep the public classification as **framework-verified, matter-specific review required** and obtain jurisdiction-qualified review before publishing any new page that states an exact right, deadline, calculation, offence, penalty, forum or remedy.
